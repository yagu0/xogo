Emergo Rules

Similar to Checkers, with prisoners stacked below capturers.

Nothing disappears in this game: captured pieces become part of the capturing unit. Therefore, a composed piece or unit will always be understood as a combination of W white (elementary) pieces and B (elementary) black ones, with W + B ≥ 1.

The 9x9 board is initially empty. Each player receives 12 stackable pieces, "in hand". At each turn, a player must pick an action among the followings:

  • Enter a new piece (W + B = 1) on the board.
  • Move a unit along diagonals, by one square.
  • Capture something.

Entering moves

Introducing a piece such that the opponent can take it on next turn is forbidden, unless another capture is already available.

At first move, white cannot place a piece at the central point.

If the opponent already placed all his pieces in hand, then a "shadow piece" enters, formed by all the remaining pieces available (thus in this case W + B > 1).

While you have pieces in hand, none of your pieces can move on the board unless they capture something.

Captures

Captures work exactly as in Checkers: by jumping over a diagonally adjacent piece to land on a free square just behind. If a capture is possible, then it must be played; in this case no piece can be introduced on the board. If after a capture another is possible with the same piece, it must also be played — except if that implies turning at 180 degrees.

Captures can be described formally as follows. If, as white, W1/B1 jumps over B2/W2 at square S2 to land on S1', then W1/(B1+1) arrives on S1' while (B2-1)/W2 stays on S2. If B2 - 1 = 0, then the jumped piece change color (becomes white). If W2 = B2 - 1 = 0, nothing remains at the captured unit location. As black: exchange W and B.

In other words, each unit is a stack of friendly and enemy pieces, with friendly pieces on top. After each capture, the prisoners part of the stack is incremented, while the jailers quantity at the captured location decreases by one.

A piece can be jumped over several times, as long as it contains at least one enemy unit (controling it).

End of the game

If no pieces of some color remain on the board (all prisoners), then the opponent wins.

If a player has no available moves but still units on board, then we can consider that the game ends in a draw.
